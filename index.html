Our journey — a story in nine pages

Happy Birthday,
my dearest Ufi

Page 1 — The Beginning

I sometimes think about how quietly you entered my life. We first crossed paths one ordinary day, with no idea that a simple hello would eventually lead us here. There was nothing dramatic about the beginning. Just two people deciding to start a conversation, curious enough to see where it might go. Then, somehow, we kept talking. Day after day, our conversations became part of our lives. At first, it was just getting to know each other—asking questions, sharing stories, talking about whatever came to mind. But somewhere in between those conversations, something changed. I began to notice your absence when you weren't around. I began to look forward to seeing your messages. You were slowly becoming part of my everyday life without either of us making a conscious decision for that to happen.

Page 2 — A Voice, Then a Face

And then we heard each other's voices for the first time. It was different from texting. Words on a screen give you time to think, but a voice carries something more. The pauses, the laughter, the tone—it makes someone feel closer in a way that messages cannot. After that, seeing each other through a video call made the distance feel even stranger. There you were. I could see your face. I could hear you. We could talk and laugh together. Yet there was still a screen between us. I think that was when I first understood the strange nature of being close to someone who is far away. Technology could bring us together for a while, but eventually the call would end. The screen would go quiet. And I would be reminded that you were still somewhere else. Maybe that was when I started to miss you.

Page 3 — Finally Meeting

So when we finally met in person, there was something almost surreal about it. After knowing your words, your voice, and your face through a screen, you were suddenly standing right in front of me. There was no connection to lose, no screen to look through, no distance to imagine. Just you. I had already spent so much time getting to know you, but being beside you made me realize that there is a difference between knowing someone and simply having them there. And strangely, having you there made the distance afterward feel harder. Because once you know what it feels like to have someone beside you, you become more aware of what it means when they are not.

Page 4 — The Waiting

We spent a long time apart after that. Almost six months passed before I came to your home and met your parents. Six months is not particularly long when you look at it on a calendar. But when you are waiting for someone, you experience time differently. There were days when I wished I could see you instead of seeing you through a screen. Days when a conversation had to end because there was nowhere else for it to go. Days when the phone became quiet and all I could do was continue with everything else. But maybe that distance was teaching me something. That love isn't always about being beside each other. Sometimes it is about staying close when you cannot be.

Page 5 — Entering Your World

And when I finally came to your home, it felt like I was stepping into a part of your life that I had only known from a distance. I met your parents. I saw the place that was yours before I ever became part of your story. There was something meaningful about that. Not because it was some grand occasion, but because I was beginning to understand that loving you also meant slowly becoming familiar with the people and places that shaped you. Not long after that, we did something wonderfully ordinary. We went to the cinema together and watched Hail Mary at IMAX. After everything that had been complicated by distance, there was something comforting about simply sitting beside you. We didn't have to call. We didn't have to stare at a screen from different places. We were just there, together, watching the same movie.

Page 6 — The Ordinary Moments

And I think that was when I started to appreciate the ordinary side of what I wanted with you. Not only the big moments. Not only the memorable days. But the simple ones too. The days that probably wouldn't sound important when told as a story, but would matter because they were ours. As I became more involved in your world, I also began spending time around the people who were part of it. I remember going to Timezone with you and your friends, walking around, laughing, just being there. It was another small moment, but I think those moments matter. Because knowing someone isn't only about knowing how they are when they are with you. It is also about seeing them with their friends, in familiar places, in the parts of life that existed before you arrived. Little by little, I was getting to know more of your world.

Page 7 — Your Dad

And then there was your dad. I had always been a little nervous around him. Not because he had to say anything intimidating, but because I knew who I was becoming in relation to you. The man who wants to marry his daughter carries a different kind of weight. Then one day, I had a flat tyre. And your dad helped me change it. It's funny how such a simple thing can stay in your memory. It wasn't a grand conversation. There was no big moment. Just me dealing with a problem, and your dad standing there helping me fix it. But maybe that is exactly why I remember it. For all the things I had imagined about meeting your family, it was a flat tyre that gave me one of my most genuine moments with your dad. I had been nervous about how he saw me, whether I was good enough, whether I was someone he could trust. And then there we were, doing something completely ordinary. Changing a tyre. Maybe I will remember that more than any carefully chosen words, because sometimes closeness isn't created by conversations at all. Sometimes it is created simply by helping someone when they need it.

Page 8 — Looking Back

Looking back at everything now, I don't think our story is made up of one moment that suddenly made everything clear. It feels more like a collection of small changes. A stranger becoming someone I wanted to talk to every day. A voice becoming familiar. A face becoming someone I wanted to see in front of me. Distance becoming something we learned to live with. A home becoming somewhere I could walk into. Parents becoming people I wanted to know. A movie becoming a memory simply because I was sitting beside you. Walking around with your friends. And a flat tyre somehow becoming a memory of your dad helping me. None of those moments, by themselves, explains where we are now. But together, they do. Somewhere along the way, you stopped being someone I was simply getting to know. You became someone whose life I want to share.

Page 9 — Now

And now, here we are. Still learning. Still growing closer. Still figuring things out. There are still places we haven't been, ordinary mornings we haven't shared, and parts of each other we are still discovering. And maybe that's what makes this moment special. Because today, you turn 25. Another year of your life has arrived, and somehow, I am grateful that I get to be here for this chapter of it. When I think about everything that has happened since we first crossed paths, I realize how much can change without us noticing. A simple conversation can become something you look forward to. A voice can become familiar. Someone far away can slowly become someone you cannot imagine your days without. And now I get to watch you become another year older, carrying everything you've been through and everything you are still becoming. I don't know exactly what the next years will look like. I don't know all the places we'll go, all the ordinary mornings we'll wake up to, or all the little memories that are still waiting for us. But I know I want to be there for them. Not just for the big moments. For the ordinary ones too. The quiet mornings. The tired evenings. The silly conversations. The difficult days. The small celebrations that nobody else will remember but us. Because maybe that's what I want most—not to know exactly how our story ends, but to keep being part of the chapters as they come. So, happy 25th birthday, Ufi. I hope this year is kind to you. And I hope that when you look back on your 25th someday, you remember that somewhere along the way, there was someone who was quietly, sincerely, and completely happy to be walking beside you. One conversation. One ordinary day. One step closer. And then another. Happy birthday, my love.

With all my love,

Renardi — Your Future Husband 🌹
